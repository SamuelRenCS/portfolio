Case study 01 · Gamification

Giving athletes a reason to open the app tomorrow.

XP, levels, streaks and Circuit Showdown, a seasonal competition between teams, built so the game rewards the coaching, not just the clicking.

Role
Designed and built it end to end: rules, data model, APIs, mobile and web UI
Timeline
Mar – Sep 2026
Stack
Flutter · Django · PostgreSQL · Next.js
Result
2× signups since January 2026
  2. 1

    Problem

    Training alone doesn’t stick.

    Athletes got a daily list of protocols and workouts. Finishing them earned nothing visible, and missing a day cost nothing. Coaches could encourage effort in chat, but nothing in the product rewarded it.

    The answer couldn’t reward volume over quality, because the product is coaching.

  3. 2

    Decision

    Make the coach’s review the scoring event.

    • Points the moment an athlete submitsPoints when a coach reviews the work

      Rewards quality, not volume. If a coach re-grades, only the difference is applied.

    • One score for everythingTwo currencies

      XP and levels for personal progress, which never reset. Circuit points for teams, which reset every season.

    • Multipliers and bonuses at launchXP for workouts and daily pillars only, for now

      Less to explain and tune before we knew what motivated athletes. The schema leaves room for more.

    Athlete submits a workout
    Points on submit
    Coach reviews scores
    Points to athlete and team 12→15 = +3
    Weekly standings weekly job
    Season leaderboard no overlap
    New leader → activity feed
    XP · levelsPersonal. Never resets. 500 XP to level 2, then +50 per level.
    Circuit pointsTeam. Resets every season.
  4. 3

    Build

    Scoring you can retry, re-grade and trust.

    • Retry-safe XP. Every award is an event with a unique key, written inside a transaction with row locks, so a retry or double tap can’t credit twice.
    • Re-grades apply the difference. 12 → 15 awards +3, so coaches can revise freely.
    • Seasons that never overlap. Every week belongs to exactly one season; a weekly job finalizes standings and posts a new leader to the feed.
    • Mobile and web. Team rankings, level progress, streaks and the home header in Flutter; circuit points in the Next.js web app.
    • Then I made it fast. An API audit found Home firing 5+ requests, one a duplicate. I folded them into one bootstrap response and cached it on disk.

    Level progress, current releaseDemo data

  5. 4

    Shipped

    Signups doubled.

    App signups have doubled since I joined in January 2026, over the same period that gamification and Circuit Showdown became the core of the product.

    The loop now does the work: coaches grade submissions that feed a live season, and athletes see their level, streak and team rank before they tap anything.

Case study 02 · Platform · Live

Turning a chat server into a platform that’s safe for teenagers.

A new Next.js web app, sign-up with guardian consent, and messaging locked down on the server, with Zulip kept as the engine underneath.

Role
Scoped it, made the architecture and product calls, built most of it
Timeline
Jul – Sep 2026
Stack
Next.js · Django · Docker · nginx on GCP
Status
Live on cortexflex.org since the September 2026 cutover
  2. 1

    Problem

    The product was still a chat app underneath.

    CortexFlex was built on a fork of Zulip, an open-source team chat server. That gave us a fast start, but the web product was still Zulip’s interface: open channels, direct messages, and a list of every member.

    Many of our athletes are 13 to 17. Open athlete-to-athlete messaging couldn’t be moderated responsibly, sign-up never asked anyone’s age, and transactional email was down on an account set up by a former developer.

  3. 2

    Decision

    Keep the engine. Replace the surface. Say no on purpose.

    • Rewrite the backendZulip as an API-only engine behind a new Next.js app

      Keeps the Cortex product we’d already built inside it.

    • Moderate athlete-to-athlete chatLock messaging down on the server

      Athletes can message only their own coach or Cortex staff. Channels and group messages are closed, and an activity feed replaces the rest.

    • SMS to guardians at launchEmail first, behind one notify_guardian() seam

      No new vendor or stored phone numbers yet; SMS can drop in later.

    • One age rule for everyoneAge bands per country and region

      A US athlete under 13 needs COPPA’s two-step guardian consent; elsewhere, sign-up below the local minimum is blocked.

    Onboarding: role, date of birth, region

    Then one of four bands

    Self-consent age 18 · 13 in the US
    Guardian band CA 13–17 · QC 12–17
    Guardian signs from a one-time link no account
    Not signed in 14 days account deleted
    US, under 13 COPPA · two emails
    Below the local minimumblocked

    Consent by age and region, as it runs today

  4. 3

    Build

    A new front door, with the keys kept on the server.

    • A Next.js app replacing Zulip’s web UI. Route handlers call Zulip on the server; the browser only holds an httpOnly session cookie, never an API key.
    • Registration rebuilt around personas (athlete, coach, parent), with a date-of-birth and region step, age rules per country, and guardian consent by signed link.
    • Consent documents per region. Canada, Québec in English and French, and the US, with consumer, team, coach and parent versions.
    • Messaging lockdown enforced by the server, not just hidden in the interface.
    • One VM, two hostnames. The Next.js app runs in Docker beside Zulip on the same GCP VM. nginx sends cortexflex.org to the app and keeps cortexflex.net for the API, so the app’s calls to Zulip never leave the machine.

    The new web app, Today pageNext.js

  5. 4

    Shipped

    Live, with every step written down.

    cortexflex.org now runs the new web app, and cortexflex.net answers only the API the mobile apps use. Athletes can’t message each other, and an athlete under the age of consent can’t join until a guardian signs.

    The cutover and every deploy since follow a runbook with a rollback, so shipping doesn’t depend on one person’s memory.
